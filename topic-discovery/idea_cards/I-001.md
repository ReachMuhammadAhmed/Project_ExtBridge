Title:                  When nobody triages the robot: outcomes of the Linux kernel test robot's performance-regression reports, and the report-time evidence that predicts them
Research question:      RQ1 (characterize) What share of the kernel test robot's performance-regression reports are acted on (fixed/reverted), disputed (noise, misattribution, expected trade-off) or ignored, and how has that changed 2016-2026?
                        RQ2 (explain) Which report-time evidence (effect size, reported %stddev, number of affected benchmarks/metrics, benchmark family, tested branch, whether functions changed by the blamed commit appear in the report's perf-profile deltas) distinguishes acted-on from other reports?
                        RQ3 (prioritize, replication-extension) Does an interpretable classical ranker over that evidence put acted-on reports ahead of others better than the robot's own gates/order, an |%change| threshold, a change/stddev rule, and a feature set modelled on Mozilla alert triage (Hmissa et al. 2026), on a time-ordered split?
RQ form:                characterize + compare-explain + prioritize
Contribution type:      empirical study + dataset (RQ1-RQ2); replication-extension of performance-alert triage to a setting without triage staff (RQ3)
